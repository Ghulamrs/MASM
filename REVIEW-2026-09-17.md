# Assembler review — Stage 1 against ml64 and the compilers' output (Fable 5.1, 2026-09-17)

Reviewed: `~/Documents/Claude/MASM` at `ffa04ad` (master), `src/*` (1,668 lines) and
`tests/`. Built out of tree with Apple clang `-std=c++14 -O2 -pthread -Wall -Wextra
-pedantic` (clean) and, on the Windows box, `cl /std:c++14 /W4 /permissive- /O2`
(clean; its objects are byte-identical to the Mac's for a04/a06/b01). Oracles: ml64
14.44.35207, dumpbin and link (VS 2022 17.14) on the Windows box; LLVM objdump on the
Mac; `cxx1.exe` and `cc1.exe` with `-arch x86_64-windows -masm=masm -S`.
Probes: 11 encoding files (6,572 instructions diffed byte for byte against ml64's
`dumpbin /disasm`), 3 link files, 63 `q` edge/refusal files, 18 command-line and
adversarial runs, and the real corpus: **681 `.asm` files** (257 cxx1 + 424 cc1), all 681
accepted by ml64, **0 accepted by this assembler**. The defect probes are in
`review-probes-2026-09-17/`, named by the ids below, each with a header saying what
both tools give. Scratch (all listings, diffs, the corpus and its objects) is under
`/private/tmp/claude-501/.../scratchpad/masm-review/`.

**Wrong bytes and wrong objects first.** Of 6,634 instructions compared, 98 differ.
Four are semantically wrong (A4: `call`/`jmp` to a data label is a direct call *to the
variable*; A5: `mov r64,[code_label]` loads code instead of taking the address); the
compilers never emit either form. One wrong object: B5, `ALIGN 32` accepted in a
section that promises 16 (ml64 refuses). Two crashes: D1, a 10,000-deep expression
kills the thread. One silent wrap: D2, integer literals beyond 2^64. Everything else
that differs is either byte-equivalent (A1, A2, A3, A6, A7), the documented short-jump
choice (A8), or a refusal. **The purpose is blocked by refusals, not by bytes**: every
one of the 681 corpus files stops at the directive layer, and with that layer
neutralised 29 of 424 cc1 files assemble and 0 of 257 cxx1 files — section C has the
ranked inventory.

Findings the review missed, met on the way:
- ml64 writes a **Static symbol only when a relocation refers to it**: `$pdata$…` labels, `$LNpush$`/`$LNprolog$` (used only in folded `DB L2-L1`) are absent from its symbol tables; `$unwind$…` and `$LNbeg$…` (targets of `DD imagerel`) are present. Item 18's symbol diff needs this rule.
- `SEGMENT`/`ENDS` nest: `ENDS` returns to the enclosing `.CODE`/`.DATA` (a `nop` after `.pdata ENDS` lands in `.text$mn`).
- A2's rule is by the literal's unsigned value (≥ 2^32 → imm64), not its digits: `00000000000000000000000000000001h` is the short form, `18446744073709551608` the long.
- ml64 refuses unsized memory for the scalar SSE ops (A2070) but takes it for the 128-bit ones; `.DATA?` with an initialiser is only its warning A4014 (refused here).
- `TEST reg, mem` puts the register in the reg field (84/85 /r), the same rule as A1's reg,reg.
- A forward `jmp lab` into another segment is ml64's A2004 "symbol type conflict"; this assembler emits a near jump with a relocation.
- ml64 keeps `.pdata`/`.xdata` for PROC FRAME in one Static `$xdatasym` with in-place addends, opened at the first FRAME in that order.
- In the normalised ml64 listings, 24 cxx1 instructions of 11 bytes (`mov qword ptr [rbp+disp32], -2`) show 10 bytes: most likely dumpbin's continuation line carries the mnemonic and `norm.py`'s continuation regex (20 spaces, bytes, end) drops it.
